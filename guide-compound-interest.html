Compound Interest: How Your Money Grows

Simple interest pays only on your original amount. Compound interest pays on your amount plus all past interest, so growth accelerates over time. Albert Einstein reportedly called it the eighth wonder of the world - and for savers, it is.

An example

Rs 100,000 at 10% per year becomes Rs 259,374 after 10 years with annual compounding - Rs 159,374 of pure interest, without adding a single rupee. Add Rs 5,000 a month and the same 10 years reach far higher, because every contribution starts compounding too.

What matters most

Time matters more than timing. Starting 10 years earlier usually beats a slightly higher rate. Frequency matters too: monthly compounding beats yearly compounding by a small margin. And the rate matters enormously over decades - which is why comparing savings accounts and funds on their real annual rate is worth your time.

The catch

Inflation eats returns. If prices rise 8% a year and your account pays 10%, your real growth is only about 2%. Always judge investments after inflation, and never borrow at high rates to invest at low ones.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest applies only to principal; compound interest applies to principal plus accumulated interest.

Is monthly compounding better than yearly?

Yes, slightly - interest starts earning interest sooner.

How long to double my money?

Divide 72 by your annual rate: at 12%, money doubles in about 6 years.
