Understanding Your Salary Slip and Tax Deduction

Your salary slip has three parts: earnings (basic pay plus allowances), deductions (income tax, provident fund, EOBI and others), and net pay - what actually reaches your bank account. The most confusing deduction is usually income tax.

How monthly tax is calculated

Your employer estimates your annual taxable salary, applies the FBR slabs for salaried individuals, and divides the annual tax by 12. That is the tax line on your slip. If your salary changes mid-year, or you join mid-year, the monthly figure is adjusted.

Why your colleague's tax differs

Two people with the same gross salary can pay different tax if their taxable components differ - for example, exempt allowances, or deductions under different heads. Compare taxable salary, not just gross salary.

Checking your slip

Multiply your monthly tax by 12 and compare with our calculator's annual figure for your salary. A big mismatch is worth asking payroll about - errors do happen, and over-deduction can be adjusted or refunded at filing time.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between gross and net salary?

Gross is your total pay before deductions; net (take-home) is what you receive after tax and other deductions.

Is overtime taxed?

Yes, overtime and bonuses are part of taxable salary income.

Can I reduce my tax legally?

Only through FBR-recognised exemptions, allowances and credits - ask a tax consultant, never hide income.
