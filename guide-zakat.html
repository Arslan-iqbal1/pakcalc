How to Calculate Zakat in Pakistan

Zakat is one of the five pillars of Islam: 2.5% of your zakatable wealth, paid once a lunar year when your wealth reaches nisab. This guide explains the calculation step by step, so you can work it out yourself and check it with our calculator.

What counts as zakatable wealth?

Cash in hand and in the bank, gold and silver (jewellery included), business stock and goods for sale, and investments such as shares. Your home, car and personal belongings you use daily are not zakatable. Add everything zakatable together, then subtract genuine debts you owe.

What is nisab?

Nisab is the minimum threshold. It equals 87.48 grams of gold or 612.36 grams of silver. Because silver is much cheaper, the silver nisab is lower in rupees, so more people qualify. The calculator uses the lower of the two values, the method widely used in Pakistan. Note: scholars differ on details - some apply the gold nisab to gold and the silver nisab to cash and silver separately, and rulings on business assets and investments vary. If you follow a particular school, adjust accordingly or ask a qualified scholar. Gold and silver prices change daily, so always update the rate before calculating - the calculator's price fields are editable for exactly this reason.

The formula

Zakat = 2.5% x (total zakatable assets - debts), provided the result is at or above nisab and the wealth has been held for one lunar year. Example: if your net zakatable wealth is Rs 3,000,000, your Zakat is Rs 75,000.

Common mistakes

Forgetting business stock, counting the same gold twice, ignoring debts, or using last year's gold price. Run the numbers twice, and when in doubt ask a qualified scholar - especially for business and investment cases.

Frequently asked questions

Do I pay Zakat on my house or car?

No, if they are for personal use. Property or vehicles held for sale or business are zakatable.

Is Zakat due on gold jewellery I wear?

Scholars differ; many say yes, gold in any form counts. Our calculator includes all gold you enter.

When should I pay?

Once a lunar year has passed on wealth above nisab. Many people choose Ramadan for convenience, but any date works.

Sources & last updated

Method: 2.5% of net zakatable wealth above nisab (87.48g gold / 612.36g silver). Prices are user-editable because gold and silver rates change daily. Last updated: October 2026.
